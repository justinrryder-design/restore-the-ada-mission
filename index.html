Boycott of the American Diabetes Association — Tracker

1,178 people have signed the Notice of Boycott of the American Diabetes Association. This page reports, in aggregate, what they hold within the ADA and what they lead outside it — and what the Association loses for as long as the boycott holds.

Read and sign the Notice of Boycott

At the ADA's 2026 Scientific Sessions in New Orleans, five senior physician-scientists were removed from the meeting and banned from it for handing out a Diabetes Care editorial — published in the ADA's own journal — about federal cuts to biomedical research. The Association said they were removed for disruptive conduct; video and witness accounts do not support that, and the day after the removals the ADA added the words “such as protesting” to the conference code of conduct it had cited. In July, ADA leadership blocked publication of an editorial and seven opinion pieces that Diabetes Care had already accepted about the events. In September an internal review — reporting to the same Board whose conduct was in question — cleared the Association, announced in a press release the Board later conceded it had issued before reading the findings. We are withdrawing our work from the ADA until its Board permits a genuinely independent, externally led review of the Association's structure and governance.

  • 28 September 2026 — the governing bylaws are still not public

    The ADA's current bylaws do not appear anywhere on its public site. The Association posts its Board of Directors and its Form 990 filings, but not the document that sets out how it governs itself — who may suspend or ban a member, under what procedure, and with what right of appeal. Peer societies publish theirs: ADCES and AACE both post current bylaws openly. Until the ADA does the same, no member can check whether what happened in New Orleans followed the Association's own rules. If you hold a copy, or have requested one and received a reply, please send it.

  • 28 September 2026 — the code of conduct was edited the day after the removals

    The Internet Archive's record of the ADA conference policies page shows “protesting” was added to the prohibited-conduct list on 6 June 2026 — one day after the five were removed on 5 June. The removals were justified by reference to the attendee code of conduct. Compare the archived versions.

Why this list exists

Background, in the words of the Notice of Boycott

At the 2026 ADA Scientific Sessions on 5 June, five senior physician-scientists were removed from the meeting and banned from it for handing out a Diabetes Care editorial about federal research-funding cuts (Washington Post). ADA leadership said they had disrupted the meeting; video published by MedPage Today and witness accounts say otherwise, and the Foundation for Individual Rights and Expression noted that no evidence supported the disruption claim. The CEO apologised five days later and promised a review.

On 13 July the ADA blocked publication of an editorial and seven opinion pieces that Diabetes Care had already accepted on what happened in New Orleans (STAT). The authors posted all eight to a preprint server instead, where they can still be read.

Senior figures resigned or issued statements. Every living Banting Medalist and every member of the ADA Emeritus Council wrote to the Board. More than 8,000 people signed a Change.org petition. On 21 July, current and former ADA members, many of them serving editors, committee members and volunteer leaders, sent the Board four demands: removal of the CEO and others involved, a genuinely independent investigation by people with no prior ADA relationship, an unconditional public apology to the five, and restoration of editorial independence at Diabetes Care (STAT).

On 1 September the ADA issued a press release saying its own Audit and Governance Committee, which reports to the Board under scrutiny, had cleared the Association's conduct, timed alongside an op-ed from the CEO defending those conclusions. STAT reported the same day that the findings were still being sent to the Board for action. Within a week the Board put out a second statement conceding it had not yet read the committee's findings.

The boycott took effect on 9 September 2026 and holds until the Board permits a review of the ADA's structure and function led by an independent academic leader.

What the boycott actually suspends

And why counting roles is the point

Signatories are withdrawing from ADA meeting planning and attendance, including the 2027 Scientific Sessions; from Board-led activity on the New Orleans investigation; and from presentations, fundraisers, grant reviews, manuscript reviews, journal management, manuscript submissions and position statements.

Every role counted on this page is work the ADA no longer has. The committee seats, editorial positions and review panels below are not credentials. They are the labour now suspended.

How complete is the ADA directory?

A test of the one external source available

Thirty-four signatories have been independently verified against public sources as holding senior academic or ADA positions. Of those, 16 appear in the ADA's public member directory and 18 do not — and eight of the eighteen are based outside the United States, which a fifty-state directory cannot list at all.

Among the US-based verified names, roughly two in three appear. The ones missing include an ADA award recipient, a former Diabetes Care associate editor, and a past president of another major endocrine society. Absence from the directory is not evidence of non-membership.

If you signed, you can correct your own entry in one message

The signature form asks for your name and titles and for your ADA affiliations, committees and editorial positions — all in free text, which is why the answers range from a full record to a single word. Nothing about committee service, abstract review, chapter work or grant panels is findable on the open web. Questions signatories are asking

Practical guidance for people holding to the boycott

What if I get asked to review a manuscript or abstract for the ADA?

Decline, and say why. Silence reads as unavailability; a stated reason is part of the point. One signatory's wording, which you are welcome to use or adapt:

Thank you for thinking of me. However, I am supporting the boycott of the American Diabetes Association. I am unable to [participate in activity] until the ADA leadership agrees to restore full editorial independence of Diabetes Care, the board of directors invites a fully independent review of events of June 5th, ADA leadership takes responsibility for their role in the removal of professional members by armed security, and the organization commits to a policy of freedom of expression and scientific inquiry.

Replace the bracketed phrase with the specific request — review this manuscript, review abstracts, serve on the panel, speak at the session.

Does the boycott cover anything to do with patient care?

No. What signatories have withdrawn is work for the organisation: ADA meeting planning and attendance, including the 2027 Scientific Sessions; Board-led activity on the New Orleans investigation; and presentations, fundraisers, grant reviews, manuscript reviews, journal management, manuscript submissions and position statements. Clinical care is not part of it.

Open challenges

Questions the Association has not answered

  • The governing bylaws cannot be found

    No current bylaws appear anywhere on the ADA's public site. The Association publishes its Board of Directors and its Form 990 filings, but not the document that defines how it governs itself. Peer societies do: ADCES and AACE both post theirs openly. Without the bylaws, members cannot check what procedure applied to the removals and bans, who had authority to impose them, or what appeal exists.

  • The rule was edited after the fact

    The removals were justified under the attendee code of conduct. The Internet Archive shows that page was changed on 6 June 2026 — the day after — to add “such as protesting” to the prohibited-conduct list. Whatever the intent, the version now cited is not the version that was in force on 5 June.

    Compare the archived versions yourself →

  • The review answered to the body it was reviewing

    The Audit and Governance Committee reports to the Board whose conduct was in question. The Board then conceded it had not read the findings before the Association publicised them.

  • Membership itself is not verifiable from outside

    The only public membership record is an opt-in directory. Tested against signatories independently confirmed to hold ADA roles, it listed fewer than half. Anyone assessing who has withdrawn is working from an undercount, and no complete roster is available to correct it.

  • Volunteer service leaves no public trace

    Committee seats, abstract review, grant panels and chapter work appear in no searchable record. The people who did that work are the only source for it, which is why this page asks signatories directly rather than relying on what can be looked up.
